Mathematical Equations: Converting A4 Reference Pitch & Logarithmic Cents

Human pitch perception is logarithmic rather than linear: the ear hears an octave doubling from 110 Hz (A2) to 220 Hz (A3) as the exact same musical distance as the doubling from 440 Hz (A4) to 880 Hz (A5). To divide each octave into 12 equal semitones (12-Tone Equal Temperament, or 12-TET), every semitone step multiplies the frequency by $2^{1/12} \approx 1.059463094$, and each semitone is further subdivided into 100 logarithmic cents (1,200 cents per octave):

f(n) = fA4 × 2(n − 69) / 12   |   Cents Offset (Δc) = 1200 × log2(ftarget / freference)

Using this logarithmic formula, we can calculate the exact cents deviation and mechanical string tension change ($\text{Tension Ratio} = (f_{\text{target}} / 440)^2$) for every historical and modern concert pitch standard:

  • A4 = 415.00 Hz (Baroque Period Pitch): $\Delta c = 1200 \times \log_2(415 / 440) = -101.37\text{ cents}$ (virtually one full semitone flat, so an A4 on a Baroque harpsichord or gut-string violin sounds like G#4/Ab4 on a modern piano). String tension drops by 11.04% compared to 440 Hz.
  • A4 = 430.54 Hz (Scientific / Sauveur C4 = 256 Hz Pitch): Proposed by French physicist Joseph Sauveur in 1713 so that every C octave is an integer power of two ($C_0 = 16\text{ Hz}, C_4 = 256\text{ Hz}, C_5 = 512\text{ Hz}$). In 12-TET, $256 \times 2^{9/12} = 430.539\text{ Hz}$, which sits at -37.63 cents below 440 Hz.
  • A4 = 432.00 Hz (Verdi 1884 / Alternative Concert Pitch): Advocated by Italian opera composer Giuseppe Verdi in 1884 to reduce soprano vocal strain compared to 450 Hz 19th-century theatre pitches. $\Delta c = 1200 \times \log_2(432 / 440) = -31.7666\text{ cents}$ (roughly 31.8 cents flat), reducing string tension by 3.60%. Note that in 12-TET Equal Temperament, A4 = 432.00 Hz places Middle C (C4) at 256.87 Hz (not 256.00 Hz).
  • A4 = 440.00 Hz (ISO 16 International Standard): Adopted at the 1939 London conference and published as ISO 16. Serves as the baseline `0.00 cents` reference for synthesizers, digital pianos, and guitars worldwide.
  • A4 = 442.00 Hz & 443.00 Hz (Modern Orchestral & MinYueTuan Standard): $\Delta c = +7.85\text{ cents}$ at 442 Hz and $+11.76\text{ cents}$ at 443 Hz, increasing string tension by +0.91% to +1.37% to project violin and wind overtones through large symphony halls.

Why 432 Hz vs 440 Hz Matters Less for Consonance Than Temperament Choice

1. The 13.69-Cent Major Third Beating Phenomenon

In 12-TET Equal Temperament—whether anchored at 432 Hz, 440 Hz, or 442 Hz—every Major Third interval (`C4 to E4` or `G3 to B3` on a guitar) is tuned to $2^{4/12} = 1.25992$, which is +13.69 cents sharper than the pure harmonic 5th partial ratio of $5:4 = 1.25000$. When two sustained instruments (such as a violin double-stop or brass section) play a 12-TET Major Third at C4 (261.63 Hz) and E4 (329.63 Hz), their 5th and 4th overtones collide and produce an audible acoustic beat frequency of $5(261.63) - 4(329.63) = 1308.15 - 1318.52 = \mathbf{10.37\text{ beats per second (Hz)}}$. Lowering A4 to 432 Hz in 12-TET merely slows that beating from 10.37 Hz to 10.18 Hz; switching to Just Intonation (5:4) eliminates the beating completely (`0.00 Hz`).

2. When to Use Pythagorean vs Just vs 12-TET on String Instruments

Fretted instruments with straight metal frets (guitars, ukuleles, and electric basses) have fixed 12-TET semitone spacing built into the fingerboard, so their open strings should be calibrated in 12-TET to prevent out-of-tune chords across different keys. Fretless bowed strings (violin, viola, cello, erhu) tune their open fifths (`C-G-D-A-E`) to pure $3:2$ Pythagorean fifths (+1.96 cents per fifth) for maximum open-string resonance, and then adjust finger placement dynamically toward Just Intonation during slow sustained chords or Pythagorean sharp leading tones during melodic solos.

Frequently Asked Questions: 432 Hz vs 440 Hz vs 442 Hz Calibration

How many cents lower is A4 = 432 Hz compared to standard A4 = 440 Hz?

Using the logarithmic cents equation c = 1200 * log2(432 / 440), A4 = 432 Hz is exactly -31.7666 cents (approximately -31.8 cents, or just under one-third of a semitone) flatter than the ISO 16 concert standard of A4 = 440.00 Hz.

How many cents higher is orchestral A4 = 442 Hz compared to A4 = 440 Hz?

At A4 = 442 Hz, every pitch shifts upward by c = 1200 * log2(442 / 440) = +7.852 cents. Many European and Asian symphony orchestras and traditional ensembles calibrate to 442 Hz or 443 Hz to increase string tension and brighten acoustic projection in large concert halls.

Does tuning to 432 Hz make Middle C equal to 256 Hz?

In 12-Tone Equal Temperament (12-TET), setting A4 = 432.00 Hz places Middle C (C4) at 256.87 Hz, not 256.00 Hz. Conversely, if you define C4 = 256.00 Hz (Scientific or Verdi pitch) in 12-TET, A4 equals 430.54 Hz. Only under Pythagorean or specific Just Intonation ratios (such as a 27:16 major sixth above C4 = 256 Hz) does C4 = 256.00 Hz coexist with A4 = 432.00 Hz.

What is the cents difference between 12-TET and Just Intonation on a Major Third and Perfect Fifth?

A pure Just Intonation Major Third has a frequency ratio of 5:4 (386.31 cents), which is 13.69 cents flatter than a 12-TET Major Third (400.00 cents). A pure Just Intonation Perfect Fifth has a 3:2 ratio (701.96 cents), which is 1.96 cents sharper than a 12-TET Perfect Fifth (700.00 cents).
