Mersenne's Law Explained: How Unit Weight, Scale Length & Pitch Determine String Tension

Published by French polymath Marin Mersenne in Harmonie universelle (1636), the acoustic equation for a stretched vibrating string relates fundamental frequency ($f$, in Hertz), speaking length between the nut and bridge saddle ($L$, in inches), linear unit weight ($UW$, in pounds per linear inch), and gravitational acceleration ($g = 386.088\text{ in/s}^2$) to mechanical tension ($T$, in pounds-force):

T (lbs) = [ UW × (2 × L × f)2 ] / 386.088   |   T (kg) = T (lbs) × 0.453592

Every variable in this equation gives luthiers and players a direct mechanical lever over fretboard playability and acoustic tone:

  • Unit Weight ($UW$) and Core-to-Wrap Ratios: For plain steel treble strings (`E4, B3, G3`), unit weight scales directly with the cross-sectional area of high-carbon steel ($\pi r^2 \times \rho_{\text{steel}}$), meaning a `.010"` string has $(0.010 / 0.009)^2 = 1.2346$, or 23.5% more mass and tension than a `.009"` string at the same pitch! For wound bass strings (`D3, A2, E2`), total mass is the sum of the hexagonal or round steel core wire plus the outer nickel-plated steel or 80/20 bronze wrap wire.
  • Scale Length Squared ($L^2$): Moving from a 24.75" Gibson Les Paul scale to a 25.50" Fender Stratocaster scale increases speaking length by only 3.03%, yet increases tension at the exact same pitch and gauge by $(25.50 / 24.75)^2 - 1 = \mathbf{+6.15\%}$. Moving from `25.50"` to a `27.00"` Baritone scale increases tension at the same pitch by $(27.00 / 25.50)^2 - 1 = \mathbf{+12.11\%}$, which is why baritones can tune down a fourth to `B1 Standard (B1-E2-A2-D3-F#3-B3)` without requiring unplayably thick bridge-cable strings.
  • Frequency Squared ($f^2$): Each semitone step upward increases string tension by $2^{2/12} - 1 = \mathbf{+12.25\%}$, while each semitone step downward reduces string tension by $1 - 2^{-2/12} = \mathbf{-10.91\%}$.

Why Plain G3 Strings Feel Stiff and How Balanced Tension Sets Solve It

Look closely at the per-string tension figures for a traditional .010–.046 electric guitar pack at 25.5" scale length: the 2nd string (`B3, .013"`) sits at 15.4 lbs, the 4th string (`D3, .026w"`) sits at 18.4 lbs, and the 5th string (`A2, .036w"`) spikes to 19.5 lbs, before the 6th string (`E2, .046w"`) drops back down to 17.5 lbs. Historically, string manufacturers assembled packs using even thousandth-of-an-inch gauge increments rather than equalized mechanical tension.

Even more noticeable is the unwound .017" or .018" G3 string. Because an unwound solid steel cylinder of `.017"` diameter has much higher flexural bending rigidity (inharmonicity) than a thin `.009"` core wrapped in flexible nickel wire, a plain G3 string resists finger vibrato and exhibits a pronounced +20 cent sharp pitch chirp when picked hard. Switching to a balanced tension set (such as `.010 - .0135 - .017 - .025w - .034w - .046w`) or using a wound .020w" or .022w" G3 string for jazz and D-Standard setups equalizes right-hand pick bounce and improves chord intonation across the 2nd through 7th frets.

Nut Slot Width, Tremolo Claw Springs & Intonation Adjustments After Changing Gauges

  1. Check Nut Slot Binding on Wound Strings: Jumping from a .009–.042 pack to a .011–.052 or .012–.056 pack increases string diameter by `.010"` to `.014"` on the low E and A strings. If a thicker wound string pinches inside a narrow bone or synthetic nut slot, it will bind when you turn the tuning peg and ping out of tune after a string bend. Apply dry graphite (pencil lead) or PTFE nut lubricant, or have a luthier widen the nut slots with gauged nut files to `.002"` wider than the string diameter.
  2. Re-Level Floating Tremolo Bridges: Increasing total neck load from 85.1 lbs (.009s) to 103.6 lbs (.010s) adds +18.5 lbs of forward pull on a Stratocaster or Floyd Rose bridge, lifting the rear of the bridge plate off the body. Remove the rear spring cavity cover and tighten the two tremolo claw wood screws clockwise by 1.5 to 2.5 turns (or add a fourth tremolo spring) until the bridge plate sits parallel to the guitar top at concert pitch.
  3. Fine-Tune Bridge Saddle Compensation: Higher-mass strings have greater flexural stiffness at the termination points, requiring slightly longer speaking length to intonate accurately at the 12th fret. Use our Online Guitar Tuner in sustain-lock mode to compare the 12th-fret harmonic against the fretted 12th-fret note and move the saddle backward slightly if the fretted note reads sharp.

Frequently Asked Questions: Guitar & Bass String Tension

How is guitar string tension calculated in pounds (lbs)?

Guitar string tension follows Mersenne's Law: T = (UW * (2 * L * f)^2) / 386.088, where UW is the string's unit weight in pounds per linear inch, L is the vibrating scale length in inches, f is the fundamental frequency in Hertz, and 386.088 in/s^2 is standard gravitational acceleration.

What is the total neck tension of a .009-.042 vs .010-.046 electric guitar string set?

On a standard 25.5-inch Fender scale length tuned to E440 Standard, a .009-.042 Super Light nickel-wound set exerts approximately 85.1 lbs (38.6 kg) of total neck pull, whereas a .010-.046 Regular Light set exerts approximately 103.6 lbs (47.0 kg)—an increase of 21.7% in total tension.

Why do .010 strings feel looser on a 24.75-inch Les Paul than on a 25.5-inch Stratocaster?

Because string tension is proportional to the square of scale length (L^2), a 25.5-inch scale requires (25.5 / 24.75)^2 = 1.0615, or 6.15% higher tension than a 24.75-inch scale tuned to the same pitch. This is why a .010-.046 set on a 24.75-inch guitar feels closer in stiffness to a .0095-.044 set on a 25.5-inch guitar.

Do phosphor bronze acoustic strings have higher tension than nickel-plated electric strings of the same gauge?

Yes. 80/20 bronze and phosphor bronze wrap alloys have a higher material density than nickel-plated steel wrap wire, and acoustic sets typically use a wound 3rd (G3) string (.024w) instead of a plain steel .017, yielding 155 to 168 lbs of total neck load on a .012-.053 Light acoustic pack.
