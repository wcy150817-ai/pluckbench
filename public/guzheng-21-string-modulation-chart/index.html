21-String Guzheng Tuning & Modulation Guide: D Major, G Major, C Major & F Major Yanzhu Bridge Position Chart (2026)

The standard 21-string Chinese Guzheng (古筝) spans four complete pentatonic octaves from the deepest wound copper-steel bass string #21 (`D2 = 73.42 Hz`) closest to the player's body up to the highest treble string #1 (`D6 = 1174.66 Hz`). Unlike fretted instruments that change key via finger placement or a capo, the Guzheng modulates between traditional keys—such as D Major (`1=D`), G Major (`1=G`), C Major (`1=C`), F Major (`1=F`), and A Major (`1=A`)—by physically sliding specific movable bridges (Yanzhu / 雁柱, literally "wild-goose pillars") left or right across the Paulownia soundboard. Acoustic Mechanics of Yanzhu (雁柱) Bridge Modulation & Circle of Fifths Logic

Why can a 21-string Guzheng switch seamlessly between five different pentatonic keys just by sliding four bridges at a time, without touching the tuning wrench inside the right-hand tuning box? The secret lies in how the Circle of Fifths interacts with a 5-note anhemitonic pentatonic scale (a scale containing major seconds and minor thirds, but zero semitones):

  • Modulating Down a Fifth (1=D → 1=G → 1=C → 1=F): In D Major (`D - E - F# - A - B`), the third scale degree (`Mi / 3`, which is `F#`) sits just one semitone below the root of the subdominant key (`G`). Raising all four `F#` strings (#4, #9, #14, #19) by +100 cents to `G` transforms the scale pitches into `D - E - G - A - B`—which is the exact pitch collection of G Major Pentatonic (`G - A - B - D - E`)! Starting from G Major, raising the new third scale degree (`B` on strings #2, #7, #12, #17) by +100 cents to `C` produces C Major Pentatonic (`C - D - E - G - A`). Raising `E` (#5, #10, #15, #20) by +100 cents to `F` then yields F Major Pentatonic (`F - G - A - C - D`).
  • Modulating Up a Fifth (1=D → 1=A): Conversely, to modulate from D Major (`D - E - F# - A - B`) to the dominant key of A Major (`A - B - C# - E - F#`), lower the four `D` strings (#1, #6, #11, #16, and #21) by -100 cents to `C#` by sliding their Yanzhu bridges 1.5 cm to 2.5 cm to the LEFT (away from the YueShan bridge).
  • Physical Distance per Semitone on the Soundboard: Because vibrating string length $L$ between the right fixed bridge (YueShan) and the movable Yanzhu bridge increases progressively from roughly 42 cm on String #1 (`D6`) to over 132 cm on String #21 (`D2`), a +100 cent semitone shift ($L_{\text{new}} \approx L_{\text{old}} \times 0.9439$) requires only a 1.2 cm to 1.6 cm rightward bridge slide in the high treble register (#2–#5), compared to a 2.2 cm to 3.2 cm slide in the low bass register (#17–#20).

Step-by-Step Guzheng Tuning & Bridge Safety Protocol

  1. Calibrate the Green Anchor Strings First (#8 A4 = 440 Hz or 442 Hz): Before sliding any bridges for modulation, open our Online Guzheng Tuner and pluck String #8 (`A4 = 440.00 Hz` or `442.00 Hz`). Once String #8 is locked within ±1.5 cents, calibrate the remaining octave A strings (#3 `A5 = 880 Hz`, #13 `A3 = 220 Hz`, #18 `A2 = 110 Hz`) using the square tuning wrench inside the right headbox.
  2. Lift Slightly Before Sliding Yanzhu Bridges: Never drag a Yanzhu bridge forcefully across the bare Paulownia soundboard under full string tension, as the bone or rosewood base can score the wood grain and cause the bridge to tip forward. Use your left index and middle fingers to lift the string upward by 3 mm right next to the bridge saddle to relieve downward pressure, while your right hand slides the Yanzhu base smoothly to its new position.
  3. Settle String Friction Over the Bridge Groove: Because a Guzheng string passes continuously over both the movable Yanzhu bridge and the fixed YueShan bridge, sliding a Yanzhu bridge to the right increases tension primarily on the right-hand playing segment while leaving the left non-playing tail slightly slacker. After moving a bridge, press down firmly on the left side of the string (just as you would for a vibrato or pitch bend) once or twice to equalize tension across the bridge groove before checking the final Hertz reading on the microphone tuner.

Frequently Asked Questions: 21-String Guzheng Tuning & Modulation

How do you modulate a 21-string Guzheng from D Major (1=D) to G Major (1=G)?

To modulate from standard D Major (D-E-F#-A-B) to G Major (G-A-B-D-E), raise the four F# strings (Strings #4, #9, #14, and #19) by one semitone (+100 cents) to G natural (G5, G4, G3, and G2) by sliding their movable Yanzhu bridges 1.5 cm to 2.5 cm to the right toward the YueShan fixed bridge.

Which direction do you move Guzheng Yanzhu bridges to raise or lower pitch?

The playing zone of a Guzheng lies between the right fixed bridge (YueShan) and the movable Yanzhu bridges. Sliding a Yanzhu bridge to the RIGHT shortens the vibrating string length and raises pitch (+100 cents per semitone); sliding a Yanzhu bridge to the LEFT lengthens the vibrating string and lowers pitch (-100 cents).

Which strings are green on a standard 21-string Guzheng?

Strings #4, #9, #14, and #19 (or #3, #8, #13, and #18 depending on manufacturer string numbering) are colored green as visual orientation markers. In standard D Major tuning, Strings #3, #8, #13, and #18 sound the note A (Sol / 5 in Jianpu: A5, A4=440Hz, A3, A2), while Strings #4, #9, #14, and #19 sound F# (Mi / 3).

Should a Guzheng be tuned to A4 = 440 Hz or A4 = 442 Hz?

For solo repertoire and western pop crossover, A4 = 440 Hz is standard. When performing in a Chinese traditional orchestra (MinYueTuan) alongside Erhu, Pipa, Dizi, and Sheng, orchestras typically calibrate to A4 = 442 Hz (+7.85 cents) for brighter hall projection.
