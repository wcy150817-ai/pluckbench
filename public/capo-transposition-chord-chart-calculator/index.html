Guitar & Ukulele Capo Transposition Chart & Interactive Key Change Chord Calculator (2026)

A capo (short for the Italian capotasto, meaning "head of the fretboard") acts as a movable nut that shortens the vibrating scale length of every string simultaneously. While singers use a capo to shift a song's key into their comfortable vocal range without relearning complex barre fingerings, studio guitarists and ukulele players also use capos to unlock bright upper-register voicings that complement a second guitarist or piano. Complete Capo Transposition Matrix (Frets 1 to 11 Across C, G, D, A & E Open Shapes)

In 12-Tone Equal Temperament (12-TET), every fret represents a chromatic pitch step of 100 cents ($f_n = f_0 \times 2^{n/12}$). When you clamp a capo onto Fret $n$, treat the capo bar as the new zero-fret nut: the chord shape your left hand forms is the Capo Shape, while the actual pitch heard by the audience, bass player, or keyboardist is the Concert Pitch Chord located $n$ semitones higher along the 12-note chromatic circle (C - C#/Db - D - D#/Eb - E - F - F#/Gb - G - G#/Ab - A - A#/Bb - B). CAGED System Dual-Guitar Voicing & Replacing Difficult Barre Chords

Beyond accommodating a vocalist's tessitura, a capo unlocks the acoustic power of the CAGED Fretboard System. Every major key on a 6-string guitar can be played using one of five open chord families—C, A, G, E, or D. When two acoustic guitars strum the exact same open G-D-Em-C shapes in the same octave register (`82.41 Hz to 329.63 Hz`), their fundamental frequencies and low-mid body formants (`180 Hz to 350 Hz`) overlap and mask one another in a recording mix.

Instead, if Guitar 1 plays open G - D - Em - C without a capo (Concert Key of G Major), Guitar 2 can clamp a capo at Fret 7 and play open C-shape chords (`C - G - Am - F` relative to the capo). Because $C + 7 \text{ semitones} = G$, $G + 7 = D$, $Am + 7 = Em$, and $F + 7 = C$, both guitars play the exact same harmonic progression in G Major—but Guitar 2's voicings sit a perfect fifth higher (`123.47 Hz to 493.88 Hz`), creating a shimmering 12-string-style stereo tapestry without requiring a single barre chord!

  • Eliminating Flat-Key Barre Chords (Eb, Bb, Ab, Fm): Keys built on flat roots normally force guitarists and ukulele players to hold exhausting full-index-finger barres across every measure. For Concert Bb Major (`Bb - F - Gm - Eb`), clamp the capo on Fret 3 and play effortless open G - D - Em - C shapes. For Concert Eb Major (`Eb - Bb - Cm - Ab`), clamp the capo on Fret 3 and play open C - G - Am - F shapes.
  • Ukulele Re-Entrant GCEA Transposition (`G4 - C4 - E4 - A4`): On soprano, concert, and tenor ukuleles tuned to `G4 (392.00 Hz) - C4 (261.63 Hz) - E4 (329.63 Hz) - A4 (440.00 Hz)`, songs in D Major or E Major often involve cramped 2nd- and 4th-fret barre shapes. Clamping a lightweight ukulele capo at Fret 2 transforms easy open `C - G - Am - F` ukulele fingerings (one-finger C and Am, two-finger F, three-finger G) into vibrant Concert D Major (`D - A - Bm - G`); moving the capo to Fret 4 yields Concert E Major (`E - B - C#m - A`).

Acoustic Physics of Capo Intonation: Why Spring Capos Pull Strings Sharp

Many players clamp a spring-loaded trigger capo onto Fret 3 or Fret 5, strum an open chord, and immediately notice sour, out-of-tune intervals—especially on the thicker wound 6th, 5th, and 4th strings (`E2, A2, D3`) or the unwound G3 string. Three mechanical variables cause capo sharp-pull:

  1. Excessive Downward Elastomer Pressure: A non-adjustable steel spring capo designed for a thick vintage neck exerts far more clamping force than necessary to seat the strings against the fret crown. Any vertical deflection behind the fret wire stretches the string over the fret crown, increasing tension $\Delta T$ and raising pitch by +10 to +28 cents. Using a micrometer screw-tension or yoke-style capo allows you to apply the minimum force required for a clean note without bending the string sharp.
  2. Capo Placement Relative to the Fret Wire: Never clamp a capo in the dead center between two frets. Position the rubber pad 2 mm to 4 mm directly behind the target metal fret wire (on the nut side of the fret) so the fret wire itself defines the vibrating termination point rather than the soft silicone pad, which would otherwise dampen high-frequency sustain and pull the string downward.
  3. Radial Lateral Pull During Clamping: Trigger capos hinged on one side can push the 6th or 1st string sideways across the fretboard as the jaw closes. Always squeeze the capo completely open, lower it perpendicular to the fingerboard, release slowly, and then pluck each open string into our Online Guitar Tuner or Online Ukulele Tuner (in Chromatic mode) to fine-tune any +5 to +10 cent offset.

Frequently Asked Questions: Guitar & Ukulele Capo Transposition

How does a capo change the key of a guitar or ukulele chord progression?

Each fret on a guitar or ukulele raises the pitch of all strings by exactly one semitone (100 cents, or one half-step). Placing a capo on Fret N raises the sounding concert pitch of any open chord shape you play by N semitones. For example, playing a G Major shape with a capo on Fret 2 produces an A Major concert chord (+2 semitones).

Why does my guitar go sharp (+10 to +25 cents) right after clamping on a spring capo?

Excessive spring tension or clamping the capo directly in the center of the fret space bends the strings downward toward the fingerboard wood, increasing mechanical tension like a mini pitch bend. Place the capo just behind the metal fret wire and re-check open strings with an online guitar tuner.

How do I replace difficult F and Bb barre chords using a capo and the CAGED system?

To play in the concert key of F Major without full index-finger barre chords, clamp the capo on Fret 5 and play open C-G-Am-F shapes (which sound as F-C-Dm-Bb), or clamp the capo on Fret 3 and play open D-A-Bm-G shapes.

Can I use a guitar capo on a soprano, concert, or tenor ukulele?

While the semitone transposition math is identical on a GCEA ukulele, full-size steel-string guitar capos have a curved fretboard radius and heavy spring force that can crush soft nylon ukulele strings sharp. A flat-pad ukulele capo or adjustable micrometer tension capo keeps G4-C4-E4-A4 intonation accurate.
